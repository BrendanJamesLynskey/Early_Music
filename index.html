A History of Early Music

Interactive Presentations · Medieval · Renaissance · Early Baroque · The Christian East

A graphical, interactive journey through a thousand years of Western music — from the unaccompanied plainchant of the early Church, through the birth of polyphony, the Ars Nova, the Franco-Flemish masters and the golden age of the Renaissance, to the threshold of the Baroque — with a parallel strand on the Christian East, whose Byzantine and Slavonic chant is as old as anything in the West. Every presentation includes synthesised audio examples you can play in the browser, links to real recordings, and hand-drawn diagrams explaining how to read the notation of the age.

How to use this collection

Each presentation is a single-file Reveal.js deck — no build step, no dependencies beyond a CDN. Use arrow keys or click to advance, Esc for the slide overview, F for fullscreen. Look for the amber ▶ Listen buttons: they synthesise chant, organum, cadences and more live in your browser using the Web Audio API — including authentic Pythagorean tuning for the medieval examples, so the pure fifths really ring. Start with Plainchant, or jump to Reading Early Music Notation for the full notation guide.

A Thousand Years of Lineage

Early music is a single, unbroken conversation. Chant becomes polyphony at Notre-Dame; the Ars Nova systematises rhythm; the English "sweetness" crosses to Burgundy; the Franco-Flemish masters carry imitation to its summit in Josquin; and the golden age fans out across Rome, Munich, Spain and England before Monteverdi opens the door to the Baroque.
